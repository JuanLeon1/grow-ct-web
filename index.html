Our Mission

"Teach people how to grow and use fresh produce — improving nutrition across CT."

We are working towards a future where everyone has access to fresh produce and the facilities to maintain a nutritious diet. Through hands-on garden education, Growing Kit workshops, and local partnerships, Grow CT bridges the gap between food insecurity and sustainable nourishment.

Herb · Easy

Basil

Fast, fragrant, and forgiving. A great first plant for a container.

Light
6–8 hours of direct sun, or a grow light 12–14 hours a day
Container
At least 8 in. wide and 8 in. deep, with drainage holes
Water
When the top inch of soil feels dry
First harvest
About 6–8 weeks from seed

Planting

  1. Fill the container with moist potting mix, stopping about an inch below the rim.
  2. Sprinkle a few seeds over the surface and cover them with about ¼ inch of soil.
  3. Keep the soil moist and warm (above 70°F). Seedlings usually sprout in 5–10 days.
  4. When seedlings have two sets of true leaves, thin to the strongest one to three plants.

Everyday care

  • Water at the base when the top inch of soil is dry. Never let the pot sit in standing water.
  • Basil hates the cold. Keep it away from drafty windows, and bring it inside if nights dip below 50°F.
  • Feed with a balanced liquid fertilizer at half strength every 3–4 weeks.

Harvesting

  • Start picking once the plant has six to eight leaves.
  • Pinch each stem just above a pair of leaves. Two new shoots grow from that spot.
  • Pinch off flower buds as they appear, and never take more than a third of the plant at once.

Watch out for

  • Yellow leaves: usually too much water.
  • Tall, pale, leggy plant: not enough light.
  • Black, limp leaves: the plant got chilled. Move it somewhere warmer.

Tip: Pinching the tips early makes basil grow bushy instead of tall.

Leafy green · Easy

Lettuce

A cool-weather crop that grows in a shallow pot and keeps giving.

Light
4–6 hours of sun. Afternoon shade helps in warm weather
Container
At least 6 in. deep. Wide and shallow is ideal
Water
Keep soil evenly moist, like a wrung-out sponge
First harvest
About 4–6 weeks from seed

Planting

  1. Fill the container with moist potting mix.
  2. Scatter seeds thinly over the surface and cover with a light dusting of soil, about ¼ inch.
  3. Water gently. Seeds sprout in 2–7 days and do best at 60–70°F.
  4. Thin seedlings to about 4 inches apart. The thinnings make tasty baby greens.

Everyday care

  • Water gently every day or two. The soil should never dry out completely.
  • Lettuce turns bitter in heat. Move the pot to shade when temperatures climb above about 75°F.
  • Feed lightly with a balanced fertilizer every 2–3 weeks.

Harvesting

  • Pick the outer leaves when they are 4–6 inches long and leave the center to keep growing.
  • Or cut the whole plant about an inch above the soil. It often regrows for a second harvest.
  • Rinse and eat soon after picking for the best flavor.

Watch out for

  • Bitter taste or a tall center stalk: too hot. Pull it up and replant.
  • Holes in leaves: slugs or caterpillars. Pick them off by hand.
  • Wilting in moist soil: too hot, or the pot is not draining.

Tip: Sow a small new batch every two weeks for a steady supply of fresh leaves.

Fruiting vegetable · Moderate

Cherry Tomatoes

The most rewarding crop in a container, with a little more patience.

Light
6–8+ hours of direct sun. Indoors, it needs a strong grow light
Container
At least 5 gallons, about 12 in. wide and deep
Water
Deeply, whenever the top inch is dry. Be consistent
First harvest
About 60–80 days after transplanting a seedling

Planting

  1. Choose a compact “patio” or “dwarf” cherry variety and a pot with drainage holes. Fill it with potting mix.
  2. Start with a healthy seedling, or start seeds indoors 6–8 weeks before moving them outside.
  3. Plant deep: bury the stem up to the first set of leaves. New roots grow along the buried stem.
  4. Add a stake or small cage when you plant, so you do not damage roots later.
  5. Outdoors, wait until frost danger has passed (usually early to mid May in coastal Connecticut) and nights stay above 50°F.

Everyday care

  • Water deeply until it drains out the bottom, then wait until the top inch dries before watering again.
  • Once flowers appear, feed with a tomato fertilizer, following the label.
  • Growing indoors? Gently shake the flower clusters every day so they pollinate.

Harvesting

  • Pick when the fruit is fully colored and comes off with a gentle twist.
  • Pick often. Regular harvesting encourages the plant to make more.

Watch out for

  • Dark, sunken patch on the bottom of the fruit: uneven watering (blossom end rot).
  • Cracked fruit: also uneven watering. Keep a steady routine.
  • Flowers dropping without fruit: too hot, too cold, or not pollinated.

Tip: A steady watering routine is the biggest key to sweet, uncracked tomatoes.

Fruiting vegetable · Moderate

Peppers

Compact plants that can keep producing for months once they get going.

Light
6–8 hours of direct sun. Peppers love warmth (70–85°F)
Container
At least 3 gallons, about 10–12 in. wide
Water
When the top inch is dry. Do not let it stay soggy
First harvest
About 60–90 days after transplanting a seedling

Planting

  1. Start with a seedling, or sow seeds ¼ inch deep indoors 8–10 weeks before moving them outside. Pepper seeds are slow and may take 1–3 weeks to sprout in warm soil.
  2. Move seedlings to their pot once they have several sets of leaves and nights stay above 55°F.
  3. Plant at the same depth as in the starter pot, in potting mix, in a container with drainage holes.
  4. Add a stake for taller varieties.

Everyday care

  • Let the top inch of soil dry between waterings. Overwatering is the most common mistake.
  • Feed lightly with a balanced fertilizer once flowers appear. Too much nitrogen makes lots of leaves and few peppers.
  • Growing indoors? Gently shake the plant now and then to help the flowers pollinate.

Harvesting

  • Cut peppers from the plant with scissors, leaving a bit of stem. Pulling can break branches.
  • Green peppers are ready to eat. Left longer, they ripen to red, yellow, or orange and get sweeter.
  • Picking regularly encourages more peppers.

Watch out for

  • Flowers dropping: too hot (over about 90°F), too cool at night, or the plant is stressed.
  • Curling leaves with tiny insects: aphids. Rinse them off with a spray of water.
  • Soft, dark patch on the bottom of the fruit: uneven watering.

Tip: If you grow a hot variety, wear gloves and wash your hands before touching your eyes.

Herb · Easy

Mint

Nearly unstoppable, but give it a pot of its own.

Light
3–6 hours of sun. Partial shade is fine
Container
10–12 in. wide, with mint planted alone in it
Water
Keep evenly moist, but not soggy
First harvest
A few weeks after planting a starter plant

Planting

  1. Start with a small plant or a stem cutting rooted in water. Mint seeds are slow and unreliable.
  2. Give mint its own pot. Its roots spread fast and crowd out other plants.
  3. Plant in potting mix at the same depth as the starter pot, and water well.
  4. Set it somewhere bright but out of harsh afternoon sun.

Everyday care

  • Keep the soil moist. Mint wilts quickly when dry, but usually perks up after a drink.
  • Trim often to keep it full and bushy.
  • Feed lightly once a month while it is growing. Re-pot or divide it once a year when roots fill the pot.
  • Outdoors, set the pot on a hard surface so roots cannot escape through the drainage holes into the ground.

Harvesting

  • Snip stems just above a pair of leaves once the plant is about 6 inches tall.
  • Pinch off flower buds. Leaves taste best before the plant flowers.

Watch out for

  • Tall, pale stems: not enough light.
  • Orange dust or spots under the leaves: rust. Remove affected leaves and water at the soil, not the leaves.
  • Tiny insects on new growth: aphids. Rinse them off with a spray of water.

Tip: Mint spreads fast. Keep it in its own pot and never plant it in the ground.

General guidance for growing in containers in Connecticut. Sizes and timing are approximate and vary with the variety, your window or yard, and the weather.
